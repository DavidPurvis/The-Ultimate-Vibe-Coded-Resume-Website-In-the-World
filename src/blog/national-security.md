You may have read that North Korean operatives have been applying to remote tech jobs under stolen or invented identities, collecting paychecks on behalf of a sanctioned regime. U.S. agencies have warned employers about it. It is a real and serious problem.

I would like to announce my contribution to solving it.

## I have also applied to every remote tech job

Every application I submit takes up one more seat in the applicant pool. That is one fewer seat for a fake candidate with a suspiciously generic LinkedIn and a webcam that is always "broken."

I am not a hero. I am a denominator.

## How it works

1. A remote engineering job is posted.
2. Hundreds of applications arrive, some of them from people who are not who they say they are.
3. One of them is me, a real person in Colorado, who is exactly who he says he is and would very much like the job.
4. The odds of a fraudulent hire fall by a small but patriotic amount.

## A note to recruiters

When you see my application in the pile, you are not looking at spam. You are looking at a line of defense.

I will show up to the video interview with my camera on, in my actual house, as myself. I will answer questions about my résumé because I lived it. If you ask about the weather in Pyongyang, I will not know, and I will not pretend to.
